## Pitch 1 — THE FERRY AT KOSTIN CROSSING  (FAVORITE)
**Logline:** On a frozen border river in a collapsing fictional republic, a retired army sniper who now runs the last ferry has one night to smuggle her deserter brother across before the colonel who trained her both arrives to cross himself.
**Genre/tone:** Cold-war-style border thriller. Hard, quiet, mean. Nobody is clean; every kindness costs something.
**Characters:**
- VERA KOST (mid-40s) — ex-sniper, ferry operator. Want: get her brother across tonight. Stakes: if caught, she's shot for treason and the crossing is burned for everyone.
- PAVEL KOST (early 30s) — her younger brother, deserter, charming liar, carrying something he stole.
- COLONEL ANTON REYD (late 50s) — Vera's old commander. Polite, patient, lethal. He's fleeing too, and he needs her ferry.
**Locations:** 1) The ferry landing (ice-choked river, cable ferry). 2) Ferry hut (stove, radio, rifle on pegs). 3) Checkpoint barrier on the river road. 4) The ferry deck mid-river in fog. 5) The far bank birch line.
**Look:** Late-1970s/early-80s Eastern European cold-war thriller. 35mm, Kodak 5247-style stock, vintage anamorphic-free spherical primes (32/50/85mm), 24fps, muted steel-blue and sodium-orange, breath vapor, hard practicals. Static locked-off for tension, handheld only when violence breaks.
**Episode 1 outline — "Ice Out"**
- Act 1: Dusk. Vera finds Pavel hiding in the ferry hut, bleeding, with a sealed army dispatch case. She agrees to cross him at midnight when the guard changes. Headlights on the river road. **[AD BREAK 1]**
- Act 2: Colonel Reyd arrives alone, civilian coat over uniform. He knows the crossing; he wants passage tonight — and he knows a deserter is somewhere near. Tea at the stove; Pavel under the floorboards. Reyd notices two cups. **[AD BREAK 2]**
- Act 3: Mid-river in fog, all three on the deck. Reyd reveals the dispatch case is his — Pavel stole his ticket out. Pistol vs. boathook. Vera chooses: she cuts the ferry cable. The barge drifts toward the far bank with all three aboard and only one gun between them. Cut to black on the ice cracking.
**Why it's good TV:** One woman, one boat, two men who both need her and both lie to her. Simple geography, rising pressure every scene, an ending that forces a choice. Military flavor is earned (deserter, colonel, checkpoint) without a single product. Cheap to make: few locations, few characters, night + fog hides drift.

## Pitch 2 — DEBT OF IRON
**Logline:** A broke private military contractor, cut loose in a desert war that's officially over, has 48 hours to sell the last working armored truck in the motor pool before the men he owes come to collect — and the only buyer is the warlord who killed his old crew.
**Genre/tone:** Neo-western war noir. Sun-blasted, cynical, darkly funny in the bleakest way.
**Characters:** DECLAN HARGROVE (late 40s), exhausted contractor, want: money and a way home; stakes: his life and his last two men. SAMIRA NADEER (late 30s), interpreter-fixer who's playing both sides. COMMANDER IBRA SOLE (50s), the buyer, courteous and terrifying.
**Locations:** Abandoned forward motor pool; salt-flat trade road; bombed hotel bar; warlord's courtyard; dawn airstrip.
**Look:** Mid-2000s desert war films. 35mm Kodak Vision2 500T pushed, bleach-bypass grade, 24-40mm lenses, heat shimmer, blown highlights. Handheld in negotiations, locked-off wides on the flats.
**Ep 1 outline:** Act 1 — payday never comes; Declan learns the company folded and he owes the wrong people. **[BREAK]** Act 2 — Samira brokers a sale; Declan realizes who the buyer is. **[BREAK]** Act 3 — at the courtyard, Sole pays in full, then asks for Declan's men as "drivers." Declan takes the money. His men watch him do it.
**Why it's good TV:** A protagonist making a rotten choice on camera in episode 1. Moral rot, sharp dialogue, big-sky visuals.

## Pitch 3 — THE NIGHT WATCH AT STATION NINE
**Logline:** A disgraced radar officer posted to a forgotten arctic listening station picks up a signal that proves her own government shot down a passenger flight — and the relief crew arriving tomorrow has orders to erase it, and her.
**Genre/tone:** Paranoid conspiracy thriller. Claustrophobic, cold, trust-nobody.
**Characters:** LT. INGRID HALVORSEN (early 40s), want: get the tape out; stakes: her life and the truth. SGT. MARCUS DELL (40s), her only crewmate, loyal or not. MAJOR ELLIS CRANE (50s), relief commander, voice on the radio, then a face at the door.
**Locations:** Radar console room; storm-buried corridor; diesel generator shed; snowfield airstrip; supply helicopter interior.
**Look:** Early-80s paranoid cinema. 35mm, Fuji-style cool stock, 40-75mm, green CRT spill, tungsten pools, locked-off and creeping push-ins.
**Ep 1 outline:** Act 1 — the signal, the tape, the realization. **[BREAK]** Act 2 — Ingrid hides the tape; Dell finds her copy; trust breaks. **[BREAK]** Act 3 — Crane lands early in a whiteout; Dell opens the door for him. Ingrid in the generator shed, killing the power to the whole station.
**Why it's good TV:** Ticking clock, two-hander tension, one great reveal per act.

## Director's pick: THE FERRY AT KOSTIN CROSSING
Strongest single-image hook (a ferry, a river, three people who can't trust each other), the cleanest plot spine, and it's the most buildable with Grok Imagine: 3 characters means 3 sheets and less face drift; night, fog and ice forgive small inconsistencies; static wides match the guide's opening-shot method. It's tough without being cartoonish, and the military edge (deserter, colonel, checkpoint) serves the story. Season engine: every episode is one crossing, one passenger, one price.

## (a) Episode 1 script — "Ice Out" (~2:30 of story, plus breaks)

**ACT 1 — EXT. FERRY LANDING – DUSK**
Ice floes grind against a flat cable ferry. VERA KOST hauls the cable by hand, alone.
INT. FERRY HUT – CONTINUOUS. Stove ticking. A rifle on wall pegs is missing. Vera stops. PAVEL sits in the dark, rifle across his knees, a leather dispatch case at his feet.
PAVEL: [smiling too fast] You still keep it oiled.
VERA: [flat, not moving] Put it back on the pegs.
PAVEL: [sets it down; winces, hand pressed to his side] I need the boat. Tonight.
VERA: [looks at the case, then at the blood] What's in the case, Pavel?
PAVEL: [beat] My future.
VERA: [long pause, turns to the window] Guard changes at midnight. You're under the floor till then.
Headlights sweep across the hut wall from the river road.
**[AD BREAK 1]**

**ACT 2 — EXT. CHECKPOINT BARRIER / INT. FERRY HUT – NIGHT**
A lone staff car idles at the barrier. COLONEL ANTON REYD steps out, civilian overcoat over uniform. He walks the river road to the hut.
INT. HUT. Vera pours tea. Reyd sits where Pavel sat. Beneath the floorboards, Pavel's eye in a gap of lamplight.
REYD: [warm, unhurried] Twelve years, Kost. You still pour like you're on a ridge.
VERA: [not looking up] You didn't drive out here for tea, Colonel.
REYD: [smiles] No. I need to cross. Tonight. Quietly.
VERA: [beat] Everyone does.
REYD: [sips; eyes drift to the table] A deserter took something of mine three days ago. He was seen on this road. [beat] Two cups, Vera.
VERA: [holds his gaze] I don't throw anything away.
REYD: [soft laugh, sets the cup down] Neither do I.
**[AD BREAK 2]**

**ACT 3 — EXT. FERRY DECK – MIDNIGHT, FOG**
All three aboard. Vera hauls cable. Reyd stands bow. Pavel hunched under a tarp, clutching the case.
REYD: [without turning] That case has my name inside it, son.
PAVEL: [throws off the tarp, laughing raggedly] Then come take it, old man.
Reyd turns, pistol already out. Pavel grabs a boathook.
VERA: [quiet, deadly] Nobody shoots on my boat.
REYD: [to Vera, calm] He sold your crossing to buy his seat. Ask him.
Vera looks at Pavel. Pavel can't hold her eyes.
VERA: [long pause; to herself] Right.
She lifts the cable axe and cuts the ferry cable. The barge lurches free into the current. Both men stumble.
VERA: [hard] Now nobody gets where they're going.
The ice groans. Fog swallows the far bank. Cut to black on a crack running across the floe.
**[END BREAK — ads]**
